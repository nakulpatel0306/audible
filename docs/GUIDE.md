# Reading Audible

What every number means and how it's worked out. All points are in your league's own
scoring. The short version, with your league's rules, is under **Help** in the app.

**Shortcuts:** `1`–`5` switch tabs, `R` refreshes from Sleeper, `?` opens Help, `Esc`
closes it.

## Gameday

**Matchup.** While a week is being played, the big number is points already scored
and "proj" under it adds projections for starters whose games haven't started. Before
kickoff the scores are pure projections. The win bar is the chance your score ends higher, from each lineup's
projected total and its spread.

**Playoff odds, bye, projected wins.** The rest of the regular season is played out
10,000 times with current rosters. Teams are seeded by wins, then points scored, the
way Sleeper does it. "First-round bye" only appears for 6-team brackets, where the top
two seeds sit out round one.

**Power rank.** Where your team ranks by rest-of-season lineup strength (see League).

**Lineup check.** Compares the lineup you have set in Sleeper with the best one by
projection, and flags byes, injured starters and better bench options. Players whose
game has already kicked off are locked, so they're left out.

**Best lineup.** The highest-projected legal lineup for the next week you can still
change, one card per starter with your bench underneath. The range under the title
covers a typical week, one standard deviation either side, and the bar on each card
shows that player's range. Click or tap a card (or its corner button) to flip it for
the details:

- **Sleeper / ESPN:** each source's projection. Sleeper's is the one Audible uses.
- **Sources split:** the two disagree by 20% or more. Those players have been less
  predictable, so their range is wider.
- **Usage up / down:** target share moved 5+ points, or carries moved 4+ a game, over
  the last three games compared with earlier in the season. Hover for the numbers.
- **Team total:** the points Vegas expects that player's team to score. It appears
  once sportsbooks post the week's lines, usually early in the week.

## Plan

**Week by week.** A card for every week left, with your odds against that week's
opponent and your projected lineup. Audible walks the season in order and, each week,
makes the pickups that week needs: a free agent who fills a slot nobody on your roster
can play (usually a bye), or a kicker or defense with a clearly better matchup. A move
has to add at least 2 points that week and can't cost more later than it gains now.
At most two moves a week. Each move carries into the weeks after it, so the drop is
gone for good. Flip a week for its moves, who's on bye, and the lineup before and after.

A week is **short** when nobody on your roster can fill a lineup slot. "Still short"
means no free agent fixes it either: that needs a trade.

**Your move list.** Every planned move in order, with what it adds that week. The plan
assumes today's free agents are still there when you need them, so recheck it after
each waiver run.

## Moves

**Waiver pickups.** Each free agent is paired with your least costly drop. "Rest of
season" is how many more starting-lineup points you'd score from now to week 17, with
later weeks counting a little less because projections get less reliable. A player who
never cracks your lineup adds nothing, however good he is.

**Streaming.** Defenses and kickers score mostly off the matchup, so Audible compares
the free agents' next three weeks to yours. It suggests a swap only when a free agent
projects at least 1.5 points better next week. For defenses, "Opp total" is the
opponent's Vegas team total: lower is better.

## Trades

**Review an offer.** Pick the team and the players on each side. The verdict comes
from how many rest-of-season starting-lineup points the deal adds for you:

| Verdict    | Your gain           |
| ---------- | ------------------- |
| Accept     | +3 or more          |
| Close call | 0 to +3             |
| Counter    | 0 to −3             |
| Decline    | worse than −3       |

Under it: what they gain, how your rank at each position in the league changes, the
single week that swings most (often a bye), and flags for injured or fading players
coming to you, or a rising player you'd be selling. For anything short of Accept,
**Counter with** tries small edits: taking one of your players out, asking for one
more of theirs, or sending a lesser player instead. It keeps those that gain you at
least 2 points without costing them more than 2, sorted by what they gain. Click one
to load it.

**Trade partners.** One card per team. "They can help at" lists positions where
they rank in the top 30% of the league and you don't; "You can help at" is the reverse.
A **two-way fit** has both, and those teams come first. The big number is your gain in
the best deal with them. Flip a card for up to two deals: every 1-for-1 through 2-for-2
trade, kept only if *both* starting lineups improve, sorted by the smaller of the two
gains. Those are the deals another manager has a reason to accept. **Show before and
after** opens the full comparison.

## Before and after

Opened from any pickup or trade.

- **Playoff odds:** before → after, both simulated from the same random seed so the
  difference comes from the move, not noise.
- **Week N lineup / Rest of season:** the change in starting-lineup points.
- **Changes the starting lineup in…:** the weeks the move matters. Most moves only
  matter in a few, like a bye week.
- **Chart and table:** projected lineup points by week, and who enters or leaves the
  lineup (moving between slots doesn't count).

## League

![Head to head: lineup strength, matchup odds, points by position and by week](head-to-head.jpg)

**Power rankings.** Teams ordered by rest-of-season starting-lineup points, not
record.

- **Position columns:** each team's starters at that position, ranked 1 (best) to
  last.
- **All-play:** the record a team would have if it had played every other team every
  week so far.
- **Luck:** actual wins minus the wins its all-play record predicts. Positive means
  the schedule has handed it wins.

**Head to head.** Lineup strength, the odds of any remaining game between you,
rest-of-season points by position, and both projected lineups week by week. It
defaults to your next opponent.
